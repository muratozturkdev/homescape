# Homescape: DESIGN.md

The single source of truth for visual and interface decisions. In code: UI tokens in `src/index.css`, 3D colors in `src/scene/kit/palette.ts`.

## 1. Art direction

**The house itself is the control panel.** A pastel isometric dollhouse in which every item carries the state of its real device. Cards float above the scene; the scene always plays the lead.

- Day: lavender tinted, bright, soft shadows. State accents (a light that is on, a TV that is playing) read through halos and a gentle glow.
- Night: navy ground, moonlight. Lamps that are on cast real light and the house looks alive. Night mode is the product's strongest moment; give it care.
- Walls read like a sketch: camera-facing exterior walls are cut low, far exterior walls stay full height; interior walls are full height by default and can be lowered.
- Objects are built from rounded boxes and cylinders, simple like toys. A clear silhouette instead of detail.
- Motion is calm and meaningful: only motion that tells state (a fan spins, steam rises, a ripple spreads, a door opens), plus short transitions that tell a change of context. Switching homes, the camera rises above the house, glides sideways and descends onto the new one (the homes behave as if side by side in one world; no overlay or text, camera motion only, about 2.6 s). Entering the Dashboard, the backdrop arrives and the cards rise slightly one after another (200-520 ms). All soft; none with "reduce motion".

## 4. Layout

```
┌──────────── Top bar 64 px: logo · home · floor · [View | Dashboard | Edit] · actions ────────────┐
│ View: [KPI][KPI][KPI][KPI]                    [map controls]   ┌── Card 380 px ──┐               │
│ Edit: [vertical toolbar]                                       │ Selected item,  │               │
│                                                                │ room or summary │               │
│                     3D house (fits the area left of the card)  └─────────────────┘               │
│ View: [Whole home] [Room 24°] [Room] ...                   room bar (full width)                 │
│ Edit: [Rooms|Lights|Climate|...|Doors and windows]         build bar                             │
│       [card][card][card][card][card] ... (horizontal scroll)   ~176 px tall                      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Margins 16 px, card padding 20 px, radius 16 px (cards) and 12 px (buttons).
- In Edit the right card ends above the build bar (`bottom 184 px`); camera framing accounts for the bar too.
- Narrow screens (< 1024): KPI tiles and map controls are hidden; the card opens from the bottom as a resizable sheet; the top bar shows icons only. In Edit with nothing selected the help card is not shown (it covered the scene); room labels hide dimensions below 640 px.
- Text in other languages can be much longer than English: buttons, segmented controls and rows truncate (…) instead of overflowing.

## 5. Component language

- **Floating card (`.glass`):** translucent surface, 14 px blur, thin border, two-layer soft shadow.
- **Icon tile (`.icon-tile`):** brand-50 to brand-100 gradient; sun yellow background when on.
- **Status pill:** green background when on or running, gray when off; below it a relative time ("3 min ago").
- **Power button:** brand blue "Turn on" when off, "Turn off" on a yellow background when on.
- **Room label (over the 3D scene):** white pill with name, temperature, humidity and a glowing yellow dot for lights that are on. Dimensions (m) in Edit.
- **Card layout:** the card body never scrolls sideways; long names and ids truncate (…). Segmented buttons and choice rows are at least 40 px tall and easy to hit.

### The scene

- **Walls:** the same thickness everywhere (10 cm), centered on the room boundary; the wall between two rooms is a single wall, and corners (the inner corner of an L included) join seamlessly. Interior walls are full height by default (like a real house, you cannot see into the next room); the brick wall icon in the map controls lowers them (brand blue when active). Camera-facing exterior walls are cut in both modes.
- **Door:** white frame, brand blue leaf, a knob in the lock color (locked alarm red, unlocked success green). Interior doors follow the inner wall setting; on the floor a brand blue quarter-circle arc from the hinge (45% line, 7% fill) and a light wood threshold. When the sensor says open, a sun yellow translucent square in front.
- **Window:** a white frame inside the wall, light blue glass, a sill inside and light brand blue curtains; when the wall is low, only a thin glass line on top of it. A floor-length window has no sill and its curtains reach the floor.
- **Shades:** curtains in light brand blue (selectable color), two panels with vertical folds on a wooden rod; a roller is a thin white tube and fabric with a dark bar at the bottom; zebra has fabric-colored horizontal stripes over translucent sheer; blinds have thin slats. Opening and closing is smooth.
- **Light spread:** each lamp lights only its own room and light never passes through walls; through an open interior door a door-shaped, widening beam of light falls into the next room; a pendant fills the whole room, wall lights and floor lamps light their surroundings more. Bulb halos are small; the room's brightness reads from its surfaces. The transition between day and night is soft.
- **Strip LEDs:** the strip itself is a continuous bright line (soft at both ends and edges), and its light on the surface it hits (wall, worktop, headboard, floor) fades softly away from the strip. Never spaced dot halos. It lights the room along the strip too.
- **Ceiling lights:** a downlight is a small white disc in the ceiling; when on, the lens lights and a soft circle of light falls on the floor (a narrow angle gives a small bright circle, a wide one spreads over the room and walls, and the visible beam widens and fades with it; "all around" lights the room like a pendant with no beam). The ceiling LED strip runs along all the room's walls just below the ceiling as a thin bright line (like a cove light), lighting the room from above with the upper walls brightest; the strip over a lowered wall is hidden.
- **Kitchen counter:** the L arm has no wall cabinets (open like an island). LED: a thin bright strip under the wall cabinets with a soft glow on the worktop, or a glow on the floor from the plinth, in the light's color; best at night. The sink is a steel bowl built into the worktop (thin glossy rim, dark strainer) with a gooseneck faucet; the steel is light gray and only slightly metallic.
- **Headboard light:** "Strip": a thin horizontal LED bar just below the headboard's top edge glowing in the light's color with a soft glow in front; "Behind": a soft rectangular glow on the wall from behind the headboard. Best at night.
- **TV and TV unit:** a white unit with light blue doors (door and body colors selectable) and thin steel handles; more doors as it gets longer. The TV is a dark-framed panel: on a thin stand on the unit, or on a dark bracket plate at eye level on the wall. The backlight is a soft rectangular glow spilling from around the panel onto the wall: in the bound light's color, otherwise Ambilight style brand blue on the left and lavender purple on the right. Most striking at night.
- **Screens:** a TV that is on plays a film: letterboxed (2.39:1), scenes with a warm and cool color split (sunset at sea, a night city, someone walking a mountain road at dawn, an evening dialogue with subtitles, a desert road), fades between scenes, a slow push-in, light grain and vignette; paused, the frame freezes with a white pause sign in the middle and a progress bar below. Monitors show a light desktop, colored code lines in a dark editor and a browser window; a portrait monitor shows a document. A screen that is off is dark glass.
- **Power strip:** a white horizontal body, light gray grounded sockets on top (two dark pin holes, steel grounding clips), a small LED by each socket (success green when on), an illuminated orange main switch on the right, a white cable curling backward. On the wall: a vertical body, sockets facing the room, the cable running down from the bottom right; it can go anywhere on the wall (into the corner) and the arrow keys nudge it.

- **Smart home devices:** calm and product-like: white or dark bodies, status lights (success green or brand blue LEDs while running, an orange glow on heaters, flickering flames in the fireplace, blinking red on detectors and sirens, a blue ripple for water leaks), a colored ring and notes on speakers while playing, a translucent light beam from projectors, a projector screen that comes down from above, interior light and a turning plate in the microwave, steam from coffee machines and kettles, a moving head on the 3D printer.
- **Boiler:** a white wall-hung body with a gray control strip below (a dark display that turns orange while heating, two steel knobs, a light), pipes with red and blue valves underneath, an orange glow in front while heating.
- **Outdoor:** gardens have a grass floor and no walls; balconies (deck) and terraces (stone paving) have a 1 m railing on their edge: dark steel posts and handrail with frosted light blue glass between. Garages have a concrete floor and walls. The house's exterior wall is full thickness on the side facing outdoor space.
- **Garden and garage items:** natural tones: trees with two-tone green leaf clusters and brown trunks (layered pine cones, a palm's curved trunk and fan leaves, an olive's gray green), wooden fences and beds, a pool with deep blue water, a light blue rim and caustic lines, white stone coping and a steel ladder; a hot tub with wood cladding, blue water and rising white bubbles; orange embers and gray smoke on the grill; string lights on a sagging wire with warm yellow bulbs; an awning in the chosen color with white stripes. The garage door has white horizontal panels and slides up to the ceiling when open. Stairs have wooden treads, white balusters and a wooden handrail.

In the right tool column under "Whole home" a roof button (a house-with-roof icon; brand blue while the roof is shown, faint when removed; it toggles between Automatic and Hidden), shortcut T. ### Sound

- Natural and mechanical sounds are realistic AI-generated recordings (Stable Audio 3); electronic beeps are synthesized. Realistic but soft: apart from alerts (siren, smoke) no sound dominates, and interface sounds are quiet and short (a dull tick, a falling whoosh). Sounds come from the device's position; in the walk they are muffled from other rooms.
- A speaker button in the top bar (Lucide Volume, Volume1, Volume2 by level; VolumeX when off) opens a glass panel (at most the screen height, scrolling): a "Sound" switch, a master level slider (mono percentage) and, under a "SOUND CATEGORIES" heading separated by a thin line, the eight categories, each with its name, percentage (mono) and switch on one line and a level slider below; a switched-off category has a faint name and a disabled slider.

## 6. Icons

Every UI icon is **Lucide** (`lucide-react`), the KPI and weather tiles included. Item kind to icon mapping: `ui/kindIcons.ts`. No hand-drawn SVG icons.

## 7. Don'ts

- External 3D models, photo textures, HDR skies.
- Decorative animation that does not tell state.
- Cards so large they cover the scene: the house must always stay visible.
- Em dash and en dash characters.
